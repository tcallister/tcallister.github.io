For every nearby and loud gravitational-wave source, there are a multitude more in the distant Universe.
Although too quiet to individually detect, when taken together these objects yield a collective gravitational-wave hum: a **stochastic background**.
The stochastic background is typically searched for in the form of a slight excess of correlated noise between widely separated detectors.
Its detection (or even nondetection) can tell us about the nature of black holes at faraway corners of the Universe in the far distant past.
